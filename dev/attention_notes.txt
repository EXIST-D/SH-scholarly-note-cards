=== method

=== section_summary sec=1
purpose: Motivates replacing recurrent sequence models: their step-by-step computation limits parallelisation within training examples, and attention is usually used only together with a recurrent network.
method: Argues from the limits of recurrent factorisation (hidden state h_t depends on h_t-1) and from prior uses of attention; then states the proposal.
result: Proposes the Transformer, relying entirely on attention, and claims it allows far more parallelisation and a new state of the art after as little as twelve hours on eight P100 GPUs.
conclusion: The Transformer is presented as the remedy to the sequential-computation constraint.
link: Opens the paper; section 2 places the idea among convolutional and self-attention models, section 3 gives the architecture.
ev: E024, E025, E026, E027
tags: introduction, motivation
=== section_summary sec=2
purpose: Positions the Transformer against models that also aim to reduce sequential computation (Extended Neural GPU, ByteNet, ConvS2S) and against earlier uses of self-attention.
method: Compares how the number of operations to relate two positions grows with distance for convolutional models (linear for ConvS2S, logarithmic for ByteNet) versus a constant number in the Transformer, at the cost of reduced effective resolution countered by multi-head attention.
result: To the best of the authors' knowledge the Transformer is the first transduction model relying entirely on self-attention.
conclusion: Self-attention is the building block that gives constant path length; its cost is resolution.
link: Follows the problem statement in section 1; leads into the architecture (section 3) and the analytic comparison of section 4.
ev: E028, E029, E030, E031
tags: background, related work
=== section_summary sec=3
purpose: Describes the Transformer architecture: encoder-decoder with stacked self-attention and point-wise fully connected layers.
method: Encoder and decoder stacks of N = 6 layers with d_model = 512; scaled dot-product attention; multi-head attention with h = 8 heads and d_k = d_v = 64; position-wise feed-forward network with d_ff = 2048; shared embeddings; sinusoidal positional encoding.
result: A fully specified model whose per-head dimension keeps the cost similar to single-head attention.
conclusion: Position information must be injected because the model has no recurrence or convolution; sinusoidal and learned encodings behave nearly identically.
link: Builds on the motivation of sections 1-2; section 4 justifies self-attention, section 5 gives the training regime used for the results in section 6.
ev: E032, E035, E043, E067, E075, E076, E084
tags: architecture, model
=== section_summary sec=4
purpose: Justifies self-attention by comparing it with recurrent and convolutional layers on three desiderata: computational complexity per layer, parallelisable computation, and path length between long-range dependencies.
method: Analytic comparison summarised in Table 1 (maximum path length, per-layer complexity, sequential operations).
result: Self-attention connects all positions with a constant number of sequential operations and is faster than recurrent layers when the sequence length n is smaller than the representation dimensionality d; restricted self-attention is suggested for very long sequences; attention heads also look interpretable.
conclusion: Self-attention is argued to be competitive or better on all three desiderata, with a side benefit of interpretability.
link: Bridges the architecture (section 3) and the experimental sections (5 and 6).

=== section_summary sec=6
purpose: Evaluates the Transformer on machine translation, ablates architecture components, and tests generalisation to English constituency parsing.
method: BLEU on newstest2014 for English-German and English-French; Table 3 variations on newstest2013; a 4-layer model on WSJ Section 23 parsing with limited tuning.
result: 28.4 BLEU on English-to-German, more than 2.0 BLEU above the best earlier models including ensembles; the English-to-French BLEU is stated inconsistently inside the paper (41.0 in section 6.1 versus the abstract and Table 2, see note F11); ablations show single-head attention 0.9 BLEU worse and smaller key size hurts; parsing reaches 91.3 F1 (WSJ only) and 92.7 F1 (semi-supervised).
